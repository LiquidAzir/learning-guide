## How to use this

Each entry gives dates, the contribution, and the chapter where it appears. Biology is the science whose credit is most often misassigned, partly because it moved from lone naturalists to large collaborations within a century, so several entries note who did the work as distinct from who was credited.

## The founders

- **Aristotle** (384–322 BCE). Dissected, described, and classified some 500 species; got embryology largely right and spontaneous generation wrong. Chapter 2.
- **Ibn al-Nafis** (1213–1288). Described the pulmonary circulation three centuries before Europe. Chapter 2.
- **Andreas Vesalius** (1514–1564). Corrected Galen's anatomy by dissecting humans. Chapter 2.
- **William Harvey** (1578–1657). Measured the heart's output and deduced that blood circulates. Chapter 2.
- **Robert Hooke** (1635–1703). Saw and named cells, in cork, 1665. Chapters 2, 3.
- **Antonie van Leeuwenhoek** (1632–1723). Ground lenses, saw bacteria and protists first. Chapters 1, 2.
- **Carl Linnaeus** (1707–1778). Binomial names and the ranked classification still in use. Chapter 2.
- **Lady Mary Wortley Montagu** (1689–1762). Brought smallpox variolation from Constantinople to England and campaigned for it. Chapter 14.
- **Edward Jenner** (1749–1823). Cowpox vaccination, 1796. Chapter 14.
- **James Lind** (1716–1794). The scurvy trial of 1747, an early controlled comparison. Chapter 17.

## Nineteenth century

- **Jean-Baptiste Lamarck** (1744–1829). Proposed evolution by inheritance of acquired characteristics: wrong mechanism, right that species change. Chapter 2.
- **Charles Darwin** (1809–1882) and **Alfred Russel Wallace** (1823–1913). Natural selection, independently; Darwin's twenty years of evidence made the case. Chapters 2, 8.
- **Gregor Mendel** (1822–1884). The particulate laws of inheritance, ignored for 34 years. Chapters 2, 7.
- **Louis Pasteur** (1822–1895) and **Robert Koch** (1843–1910). Disproved spontaneous generation; founded germ theory and the methods for proving a microbe causes a disease. Chapters 2, 10.
- **Rudolf Virchow** (1821–1902). "Every cell from a cell"; cellular pathology. Chapter 2.
- **Claude Bernard** (1813–1878). The constancy of the internal environment. Chapter 12.
- **Friedrich Wöhler** (1800–1882) and **Eduard Buchner** (1860–1917). Killed vitalism, with urea and with cell-free fermentation. Chapter 2.
- **Benjamin Gompertz** (1779–1865). The exponential law of mortality with age. Chapter 15.
- **William Beaumont** (1785–1853) and **Alexis St. Martin** (1802–1880). The physiology of digestion, through a wound; one was the investigator and the other was the subject. Chapter 12.

## The gene and the molecule

- **Thomas Hunt Morgan** (1866–1945) and the fly room, including **Calvin Bridges** and **Alfred Sturtevant**. Genes on chromosomes, in linear order. Chapter 2.
- **Nettie Stevens** (1861–1912). Identified the sex chromosomes in 1905, working on beetles at Bryn Mawr, independently of E. B. Wilson. Chapter 2.
- **Ronald Fisher** (1890–1962), **J. B. S. Haldane** (1892–1964), **Sewall Wright** (1889–1988). Made evolution mathematical and joined it to Mendel. Chapters 2, 8. Fisher also questioned Mendel's data and was a proponent of eugenics, as were several founders of statistics.
- **Barbara McClintock** (1902–1992). Discovered transposable elements in maize in the 1940s; disbelieved for decades; Nobel 1983. Chapter 6.
- **Oswald Avery** (1877–1955), **Colin MacLeod**, **Maclyn McCarty**. Showed DNA carries heredity, 1944; never received a Nobel Prize. Chapter 2.
- **Rosalind Franklin** (1920–1958). X-ray crystallography of DNA; her Photograph 51 and unpublished data were shown to Watson and Crick without her knowledge, and she died at 37, four years before the prize. Chapter 2.
- **James Watson** (1928–2025), **Francis Crick** (1916–2004), **Maurice Wilkins** (1916–2004). The double helix, 1953. Chapters 2, 6.
- **Frederick Sanger** (1918–2013). Sequenced the first protein and invented DNA sequencing; two Nobel Prizes. Chapters 2, 18.
- **Marshall Nirenberg** (1927–2010), **Har Gobind Khorana** (1922–2011), **Robert Holley** (1922–1993). Cracked the genetic code. Chapter 6.
- **François Jacob** (1920–2013) and **Jacques Monod** (1910–1976). Gene regulation. Chapter 6.
- **Christian Anfinsen** (1916–1995). A protein's sequence determines its fold. Chapter 4.
- **Lynn Margulis** (1938–2011). Endosymbiotic origin of mitochondria and chloroplasts; rejected by fifteen journals. Chapter 3.
- **Carl Woese** (1928–2012). Discovered the archaea and the three-domain tree, from ribosomal RNA. Chapters 2, 9.
- **Motoo Kimura** (1924–1994). The neutral theory of molecular evolution. Chapter 8.
- **Peter Mitchell** (1920–1992) and **Jennifer Moyle** (1921–2016). Chemiosmosis, worked out in a private laboratory in Cornwall. Chapter 5.
- **Katalin Karikó** (b. 1955) and **Drew Weissman** (b. 1959). Made mRNA usable in humans, through decades of unfashionable work; Nobel 2023. Chapters 14, 18.

## Systems and organisms

- **Alan Hodgkin** (1914–1998) and **Andrew Huxley** (1917–2012). The quantitative theory of the nerve impulse. Chapter 13.
- **Donald Hebb** (1904–1985), **Timothy Bliss** (b. 1940), **Terje Lømo** (b. 1935). The synaptic basis of learning. Chapter 13.
- **Brenda Milner** (b. 1918) and **Henry Molaison** (1926–2008). Established that memory is multiple systems; one was the neuropsychologist and the other the patient who could not remember her. Chapter 13.
- **Elizabeth Loftus** (b. 1944). Memory is reconstructive; false memories can be implanted. Chapter 13.
- **Hans Spemann** (1869–1941) and **Hilde Mangold** (1898–1924). The organizer experiment; she performed it and died before the prize. Chapter 15.
- **Christiane Nüsslein-Volhard** (b. 1942) and **Eric Wieschaus** (b. 1947). Found the genes that build an animal; Nobel 1995 with **Edward Lewis**. Chapter 15.
- **Shinya Yamanaka** (b. 1962). Turned adult cells back into stem cells with four factors. Chapter 15.
- **Cynthia Kenyon** (b. 1954) and **Thomas Johnson** (1947–2023). Single genes that double lifespan in worms. Chapter 15.
- **Susumu Tonegawa** (b. 1939). How antibody diversity is generated. Chapter 14.
- **Frank Macfarlane Burnet** (1899–1985). Clonal selection. Chapter 14.
- **Shimon Sakaguchi** (b. 1951), **Mary Brunkow** (b. 1961), **Fred Ramsdell** (b. 1960). Regulatory T cells and peripheral tolerance; Nobel 2025. Chapter 14.
- **James Allison** (b. 1948) and **Tasuku Honjo** (b. 1942). Checkpoint blockade in cancer; Nobel 2018. Chapters 14, 17.
- **Barry Marshall** (b. 1951) and **Robin Warren** (1937–2024). *H. pylori* causes ulcers; Marshall drank the culture. Chapter 10.
- **Henrietta Lacks** (1920–1951). Her cells, taken without consent, built modern cell biology. Chapter 3.

## Ecology and evolution in the field

- **Robert Paine** (1933–2016). Keystone species, by removing starfish. Chapter 16.
- **Peter Grant** (b. 1936) and **Rosemary Grant** (b. 1936). Forty years of measuring finches; evolution observed. Chapter 8.
- **Rachel Carson** (1907–1964). *Silent Spring* (1962), which showed that pesticides accumulate through food chains and began modern environmentalism. Chapter 16.
- **E. O. Wilson** (1929–2021). Ants, island biogeography, sociobiology, and biodiversity as a cause. Chapter 8.
- **William Hamilton** (1936–2000) and **Robert Trivers** (b. 1943). Kin selection and reciprocal altruism. Chapter 8.
- **Stephen Jay Gould** (1941–2002) and **Niles Eldredge** (b. 1943). Punctuated equilibrium; Gould also the period's most-read critic of adaptationism. Chapters 8, 9.
- **Norman Borlaug** (1914–2009). Wheat breeding that fed hundreds of millions. Chapter 11.

## The present

- **Jennifer Doudna** (b. 1964) and **Emmanuelle Charpentier** (b. 1968). Programmable CRISPR editing; Nobel 2020. Chapter 18.
- **Feng Zhang** (b. 1981). Made CRISPR work in human cells, in 2013. Chapter 18.
- **George Church** (b. 1954). Genome engineering at scale, cheap sequencing methods, and the long project of writing genomes rather than only reading them. Chapter 18.
- **David Liu** (b. 1973). Base editing and prime editing. Chapter 18.
- **David Baker** (b. 1962), **Demis Hassabis** (b. 1976), **John Jumper** (b. 1985). Protein design and structure prediction; Nobel in Chemistry 2024. Chapters 4, 18.
- **Victor Ambros** (b. 1953) and **Gary Ruvkun** (b. 1952). MicroRNAs; Nobel 2024. Chapter 6.
- **Svante Pääbo** (b. 1955). Ancient DNA and the Neanderthal genome; Nobel 2022. Chapter 9.
- **Kiran Musunuru** (b. 1974). Led the bespoke base-editing treatment of one infant, 2025. Chapter 18.
- **Ron Milo** and **Rob Phillips**. Made biology quantitative for students, through *Cell Biology by the Numbers* and the biomass census. Chapters 3, 16.
- **John Ioannidis** (b. 1965). Showed how much published research is false, and became a case study himself in how a critic's judgment can fail in a live crisis. Chapter 17.

## The wrong turns worth remembering

- **Trofim Lysenko** (1898–1976). Rejected Mendelian genetics on ideological grounds, controlled Soviet biology for thirty years, had geneticists imprisoned and executed, and contributed to famines. The clearest case of what happens when a state decides what biology may conclude.
- **The eugenics movement** (roughly 1880–1945), which counted among its advocates several founders of statistics and genetics, and which produced forced sterilization laws in dozens of jurisdictions, immigration restrictions, and, in its extreme form, a justification for mass murder. It was bad science, in that it misunderstood heritability (chapter 7), and its influence on the field's early leaders is a matter of record rather than of retrospective judgment.
- **He Jiankui** (b. 1984). Edited human embryos in 2018 in violation of every relevant norm; imprisoned; the children carry the edits. Chapter 18.
