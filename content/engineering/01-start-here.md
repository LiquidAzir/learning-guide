## A kettle is a system

You want hot water. A kettle turns electrical energy into heat, transfers that heat into water, contains the water safely, detects when to stop, and survives repeated use. It must also be affordable to make, easy to clean, and difficult to misuse.

Physics explains constraints on this process. Engineering chooses a workable design within them. A more powerful heater shortens heating time but changes electrical requirements. A lighter wall saves material but may lose stiffness. A sophisticated sensor may offer precision while adding cost and failure modes.

**Engineering** combines analysis, design, testing, and judgment to meet a need under constraints. There is rarely one design that is best at everything. You compare trade-offs and make the assumptions visible.

## Follow the chain

Start with requirements, units, and estimates. Then study how structures carry loads, how materials fail, and how machines transmit motion. Fluids and heat explain pumps, pipes, cooling, and engines. Circuits and power systems explain how energy travels and changes form. Feedback explains how a machine maintains a goal while conditions change.

Manufacturing closes the loop: a design that works once in a laboratory is different from one that can be produced consistently, inspected, repaired, and retired responsibly. The final chapter brings these ideas together through system reliability and emerging research.

The [physics guide](#/physics) develops the underlying laws. Here the focus is what those laws imply for a decision: which dimension to change, what to measure, which failure to anticipate, and when a model has left out something important.

:::key Four questions for any object
What does it need to do? Where do its forces and energy go? How does it detect or tolerate a problem? How will we know the design actually meets its requirements?
:::

The worked examples are simplified models for understanding. Their boundary conditions are stated because changing a support, a temperature, or an operating environment can change the answer. Real specifications require the appropriate standards, qualified design, and testing for the application.
