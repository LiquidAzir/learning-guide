## Eligible, but not enrolled

A person qualifies for a benefit but never receives it. Perhaps they do not know it exists, believe they are ineligible, cannot complete the forms, or face a long wait. Eligibility rules define only one part of a policy's operation.

**Bureaucracy** is administration through specialized roles, procedures, records, and authority. It can provide consistency and expertise; it can also produce rigidity or shield poor performance. Understanding it requires looking at tasks, incentives, information, and resources rather than treating the term as an insult.

## Rules and discretion

**Street-level administrators**—teachers, inspectors, caseworkers, and others interacting directly with the public—interpret rules under real constraints. More detailed rules can reduce arbitrary variation but may handle unusual cases badly. More discretion can improve adaptation but requires appropriate training, oversight, and recourse.

**Administrative burden** includes learning costs, compliance costs, and psychological costs involved in interacting with the state. Reducing a form from ten pages to two may help, but only if the remaining requirements, documentation, and decision process are workable.

## A queue can overwhelm good intentions

Suppose an office receives 120 applications per day but can finish 100. Its backlog grows by about 20 each day before accounting for withdrawals or resubmissions. Better staff motivation alone cannot erase a persistent capacity gap. The office must change arrivals, processing time, resources, or requirements.

A target such as “close 100 cases” may encourage staff to prioritize easy cases or reject difficult ones quickly. Measure timeliness alongside accuracy, accessibility, and resolution, so one target does not quietly displace the service's purpose.

## Recruitment and institutional history

Civil-service systems attempt to separate some public employment decisions from patronage through qualifications, procedures, and protections. Their actual effects depend on implementation. A historical study of American municipalities finds that organized public employees helped promote civil-service adoption, adding an institutional explanation beyond reform by political elites alone.[^1]

Recent field experiments on communication and service delivery test specific administrative barriers. A letter increasing applications would not by itself establish better health, income, or satisfaction; each downstream outcome requires its own evidence.

:::try A faster rejection
After introducing a speed target, an agency's average processing time falls, but appeals and overturned denials rise. Has performance unambiguously improved?

:::answer Show the reasoning
No. The target improved one measure while accuracy or fairness may have worsened. Examine the full path: processing time, correct decisions, access, appeals, and final resolution. The agency's objective is not merely to finish records quickly.
:::
:::
