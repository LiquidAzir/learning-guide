## The name that will not come

You recognize someone immediately. You remember where you met, the conversation, perhaps an embarrassing thing you said. Their name is missing. An hour later, while doing something else, it arrives.

That small failure separates several things we casually call memory. Recognizing a face, retrieving a name, and remembering an episode involve different operations. Your confidence that the name exists is another judgment again. Psychology begins by turning an everyday experience into questions specific enough to investigate.

**Psychology** studies behavior and mental processes. **Cognitive science** brings psychology into conversation with neuroscience, computer science, linguistics, philosophy, and anthropology. **Neuroscience** investigates the nervous system, from membranes to circuits to behavior. These perspectives overlap, but an explanation at one level does not automatically replace the others.[^1]

A neuron can explain how an electrical signal travels. A learning model can explain why feedback changes a choice. A social explanation can explain why someone hides that choice from a group. A useful account may need all three.

## The route

Begin with evidence: how to measure an unseen process and distinguish a cause from a coincidence. Then follow signals through neurons and networks into perception, attention, learning, and decision-making. Development and social behavior show how a mind changes through interaction with its surroundings.

The final stretch deals directly with sleep, consciousness, and the research frontier. It separates being awake, being responsive, having an experience, and being able to describe one. Those distinctions matter when interpreting experiments, clinical observations, and claims about artificial minds.

The existing [biology chapter on the brain](#/biology/brain) provides a compact biological overview. This subject spends more time on experiments, psychological mechanisms, and competing explanations. The [AI guide](#/ai) offers a useful comparison, but success at a task is not by itself evidence that a machine solves it as a person does.

:::key Three questions to carry
What exactly did the participant do? What hidden process could produce that behavior? What observation would distinguish that explanation from an alternative?
:::

## Try the experience before reading the explanation

For the practice questions, make a prediction before opening the answer. A surprising result is easier to understand when you know which expectation it overturned. Keep separate notes for an established finding, a proposed mechanism, and an unresolved question. The research feed uses the same distinctions, with special care around consciousness and brain decoding.
