## Finding words in a stream

Ordinary speech does not arrive with spaces between every word. A learner must discover useful boundaries while also learning sounds, meanings, and social intentions. The input is messy, but it contains statistical regularities and occurs in an interactive world.

In a classic experiment, infants detected regularities in a briefly presented artificial speech stream. This demonstrated a learning capacity under controlled conditions; it did not show that word-frequency statistics alone explain language acquisition.[^1] Communication also requires connecting expressions to situations and people, generalizing grammatical patterns, and learning how a speaker's intention can go beyond literal words.

## Development is a changing interaction

Genes influence how systems develop; experience changes those systems and the environments they subsequently seek. **Gene–environment interaction** means that an environmental effect depends on genetic variation, or vice versa. **Gene–environment correlation** means that genetic differences become associated with different environments. These are distinct from simply adding a “nature percentage” to a “nurture percentage.”

A **sensitive period** is a time when particular experiences have especially strong effects. Its existence does not imply that later learning is impossible. Different systems have different developmental trajectories; there is no single birthday on which the brain becomes permanently finished.

## What heritability does not say

**Heritability** describes variation in a trait statistically associated with genetic variation in a particular population and environment under a model. It does not divide one person's trait into genetic and environmental parts. A highly heritable characteristic may still respond to environmental change.

Imagine two classrooms where everyone in each class receives the same instruction, but the instruction differs greatly between classrooms. A within-class analysis cannot estimate the effect of the instructional difference because that difference does not vary within the class. The broader lesson applies to many comparisons: an analysis cannot identify effects for variation it never observes.

## Measuring differences fairly

A test score is a measurement under particular conditions. **Reliability** asks how consistently it measures; **validity** asks whether its interpretation is justified for a purpose. A consistent measure can still be a poor basis for a particular decision. Check language demands, familiarity, access, and whether scores predict the outcome being claimed.

:::try Fixed by inheritance?
A study reports high heritability for a learning-related trait. Someone concludes that better teaching cannot help. What step in that argument fails?

:::answer Show the reasoning
The conclusion confuses sources of variation within the studied population with responses to a new intervention. Heritability does not estimate the effect of improved teaching and does not establish immutability.
:::
