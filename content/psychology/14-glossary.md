## Evidence

**Operational definition.** A specification of how a concept will be measured in a particular study; not a guarantee that the measure captures everything the concept means.

**Decision criterion.** The threshold used to classify evidence, such as deciding when a faint signal counts as seen.

**Sensitivity.** Ability to distinguish signal from noise under a measurement model, separate from willingness to report a signal.

**Confound.** A factor that can provide an alternative explanation for an observed relationship.

**Effect size.** The magnitude of an estimated difference or relationship; distinct from statistical significance.

**Preregistration.** Recording a study's planned questions, outcomes, and analyses before observing the relevant results.

**Reverse inference.** Inferring a mental process from an observed brain signal; its strength depends on how selectively the signal predicts that process.

## Neural mechanisms

**Action potential.** A regenerating electrical spike produced through voltage-dependent membrane currents.

**Synapse.** A junction through which one neuron influences another cell, often by releasing a chemical transmitter.

**Plasticity.** Change in a nervous system's structure or function with activity, development, or experience.

**Connectome.** A map of connections within a nervous system or sampled part of it. It is not a complete description of ongoing activity.

**Functional connectivity.** Statistical dependence between measured signals, which need not imply a direct anatomical connection.

## Learning and behavior

**Working memory.** Maintaining and manipulating information for a current task.

**Episodic memory.** Memory for experienced events and their context.

**Semantic memory.** Knowledge of concepts, facts, and meanings.

**Prediction error.** The difference between an obtained outcome and an expected outcome in a learning model.

**Negative reinforcement.** Removal of an aversive consequence that strengthens the behavior producing its removal.

**Heritability.** A model-dependent estimate of genetic contributions to variation in a trait within a population and environment; not the genetic fraction of an individual.

**Normative influence.** Changes in behavior motivated by approval, acceptance, or avoidance of social sanctions.

## Consciousness

**Arousal.** Regulation and level of wakefulness.

**Awareness.** Experience of oneself or the environment; inferred through imperfect measures.

**Phenomenal consciousness.** Subjective experience: what a state feels like.

**Access consciousness.** Availability of information for reasoning, report, and deliberate action.

**Neural correlate of consciousness.** A neural condition associated with a conscious experience under specified conditions, requiring separation from prerequisites and consequences.

**Adversarial collaboration.** A study designed with proponents of competing explanations who agree on predictions and procedures before the results are known.

## Before sharing a brain headline

Identify the sample, task, comparison, measurement, uncertainty, and alternative explanation. Check whether a result concerns a single participant, another species, a particular state, or a broad population. Ask whether a successful prediction establishes a mechanism, and whether a proposed mechanism establishes anything about subjective experience. Often these are three different claims.
