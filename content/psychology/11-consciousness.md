## Seeing red and describing red

You can discriminate a color, name it, remember it, and use it to make a decision. There is also what seeing it feels like. Explaining the capacities and explaining the experience are related projects, but whether the first completely explains the second is a live philosophical dispute.[^1]

**Phenomenal consciousness** refers to subjective experience. **Access consciousness** concerns information being available for reasoning, report, and deliberate action. Researchers must specify which target an experiment addresses. A measure of report availability cannot settle every question about experience by definition.

## Competing proposals

**Global neuronal workspace theory** proposes that some information becomes widely available through interactions among brain systems, supporting flexible use and report. Particular versions predict characteristic large-scale activity patterns when information reaches conscious access.

**Integrated information theory** begins with proposed properties of experience and connects them to a system's intrinsic causal organization. Its mathematical claims concern how that organization is integrated and differentiated. Popular measures of signal complexity are not automatically measurements of the theory's full mathematical quantity.

**Higher-order theories** connect consciousness to representations of one's own mental states. **Recurrent-processing accounts** emphasize feedback within sensory processing. These proposals differ in scope and implementation. They should not be reduced to competing names for the same observation, nor treated as established winners.

## Correlates, prerequisites, and consequences

A **neural correlate of consciousness** is a neural condition associated with a particular conscious experience under specified conditions. The observed activity may nevertheless include preparation, attention, memory, or the act of reporting. A signal that occurs before a verbal answer could enable the experience, constitute part of it, or support describing it afterward.

“No-report” experiments reduce some reporting demands, but must infer experience from other indicators. They exchange one set of assumptions for another. Strong evidence comes from converging methods and predictions that competing theories genuinely disagree about.

In a preregistered adversarial collaboration, proponents of workspace and integrated-information theories agreed on divergent predictions before testing them. The results supported some predictions and challenged important commitments of both. The study did not settle consciousness or show that the remaining theories were thereby true.[^2]

:::try A theory that never loses
A theory predicts a frontal signal. When none appears, its advocate says the signal must exist in an unmeasured form, without specifying how to detect it. What would make the revised claim scientifically useful?

:::answer Show the reasoning
Specify the new signal, the conditions for observing it, and a result that would count against the revision. A revision can be productive, but it needs a test that could distinguish it from alternatives rather than merely accommodate the result already obtained.
:::
:::

## What remains open

Identifying reliable mechanisms and deciding what counts as an explanation are both unfinished. Precision helps: ask which experience, which organism, which task, which prediction, and which alternative.
